# CAN-SPAM checklist for the sender

This plugin never sends anything. The user sends. Before they send the first note to a US recipient, go through this list. This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice. For anything unclear, ask a lawyer.

The US CAN-SPAM Act covers commercial email, including one-to-one cold outreach that promotes a product or service. Other countries have stricter rules (for example, many require prior consent). If a recipient is outside the US, check the local rules first.

## Checklist

1. **Honest sender.** The From name and address are really the sender's. No fake or borrowed identity.
2. **Honest subject.** The subject matches the content. No "Re:" on a first message, no fake urgency, no trick.
3. **Physical address.** The message includes a valid postal address for the sender (a street address, a P.O. box registered with the post office, or a private mailbox).
4. **Opt-out line.** The message tells the person how to stop hearing from the sender. For example: "If you would rather not hear from me, reply 'no thanks' and I will not write again."
5. **Honor opt-outs.** Remove anyone who opts out within 10 business days, and do not email them again. Keep a simple list. The `exclusions.csv` file in the user's lead-finder folder can hold it (kind `email`, reason "opted out").
6. **Ad disclosure when it applies.** If the message is an advertisement, say so clearly. A personal note that offers a service is still commercial; if in doubt, make the purpose clear in the first lines.
7. **No scraped or bought lists of unknown origin.** These leads come from the organization's own public pages and work-email lookups, and each row records its source. Keep that record.
8. **Responsibility.** The sender is responsible for what goes out, even if a tool or a helper wrote the draft.

## Good practice beyond the law

- Send to work addresses only. Do not use personal addresses.
- One note, one person, one organization. No blasts.
- Send in small batches from a normal mailbox, not a bulk tool.
- Wait before a follow-up and keep it to one or two.
- If someone says stop, stop, even if the law would allow more.

## Not legal advice

This summary is general information. It does not cover every country or situation.
